Drop in, or book a time?

Office hours are the quickest way to talk with Dr. Eisenlohr-Moul, and most questions fit there. Some conversations are better scheduled. Use whichever fits.

Drop in · Wednesdays, 3–4 pm Central

Office hours suit

  • Students and prospective trainees at any level asking about joining the lab or about a project. Medical students: read medical student opportunities first, then come.
  • Clinicians seeking a general consultation on PMDD, premenstrual worsening of a psychiatric condition, or cycle-related suicidality. For a specific case, see the next card.
  • Researchers with a methods question: aligning daily data to the cycle, diary design, hormone timing, or the lab’s menstrualcycleR package.
  • Industry, advisory, and media conversations that fit in about twenty minutes.

Plan on about twenty minutes, in a room where other lab members or visitors may be present unless you ask to be seen alone.

What to expect

Join at any point in the hourOpen the Zoom link between 3:00 and 4:00 pm Central. You will land in the waiting room. People are admitted in the order they arrive, so arriving earlier in the hour means a shorter wait.
Wait to be admittedDr. Eisenlohr-Moul admits one person or group at a time, in arrival order. If a conversation is under way, you may wait a few minutes. If the hour ends before your turn, come back the following week or book on Calendly.
Ask your questionOpen with the question itself, then the context. Most conversations run about twenty minutes. When you are done, the next person is admitted.
Other people may be present. Lab members sometimes sit in, and whoever is in the room can hear the conversation. If you would rather talk alone, say so when you are admitted, or mark “Prefer 1:1” on the heads-up board, and you will be seen one-to-one.
Groups are welcome. Several people with a shared question can join together and are admitted as one. If you use the board, put all your names in one row.
Come with a specific question. The hour goes further when each conversation starts from a concrete question. A fully formed project is not expected.
Check the board the morning of. When a Wednesday is cancelled, the notice is written on the heads-up board in that week’s block. This page does not change from week to week.

The heads-up board (optional)

The board is a shared Google Sheet with a block of rows for each Wednesday. Adding your name is optional and does not reserve a place; admission is still in arrival order. It lets Dr. Eisenlohr-Moul see who may come and what they want to discuss, and it lets you see what is already on the list for the week.

The board is public. Anyone with the link can read it, and edit it, without signing in. Write only what you are comfortable with others reading: a general topic rather than details, and never patient information. For anything confidential, leave the board blank and book a one-to-one.
